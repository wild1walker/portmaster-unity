# 3D games: what costs, and the tools that cut it

The ports so far were 2D (Hollow Knight, Blasphemous, Cuphead, Enter the Gungeon): a frame of
sprites, one camera, no shadows. A 3D game's frame is made of other things, and on the handhelds'
Mali-G31 MP2 (two shader cores, about 650 MHz, no fast depth-only rendering) and four Cortex-A35 cores
each of them has a price. This page is the plan for the tools that find those prices and cut them,
started with Bomb Rush Cyberfunk (Unity 2021.3, built-in pipeline, Post Processing Stack v2, toon
shading with outlines, an open city). The rules of every port hold
([porting.md](porting.md), "Rules for every port"): the game play never changes; a picture change only
where no one sees it ([quality.md](quality.md), "Picture changes no one sees"); every speedup on by
default on every handheld.

## Where a 3D frame goes

| cost | on the CPU (Unity's main and render threads) | on the GPU |
|---|---|---|
| draw calls | culling, sorting, state per renderer and material pass; the Mali driver works in the draw call ([performance.md](performance.md)) | vertex shading per pass |
| extra passes | each additional lit pass (ForwardAdd: a pixel light), a second outline pass, shadow casters drawn once more per cascade | the same vertices again |
| shadows | the shadow casters culled and drawn into the map | the map (fill), then a lookup per lit pixel; screen-space shadows add a depth pass and a full-screen pass |
| the camera depth texture | when an effect needs it (AO, depth of field, soft particles, fog, motion blur): every opaque renderer drawn once more | another full pass of vertex work |
| post effects | little | full-screen passes at the window's size: bloom (a chain of downsamples), AO, colour grading, FXAA |
| skinning | CPU skinning (each vertex every frame) or GPU skinning (transform feedback / compute) | vertex work |
| overdraw | | transparent particles, foliage, UI |
| far geometry | more renderers to cull | triangles smaller than pixels |

## The tools

1. **`pmu-dev census3d <Game_Data>`** (done, devtools/src/census3d.rs): the game's data before it runs.
   Per scene: renderers (skinned, shadow casters, static-batched, lightmapped), triangles,
   LOD groups, lights by kind and shadows, cameras (far plane, path, HDR, MSAA), reflection probes,
   particles, animators by culling mode, occlusion data, lightmaps. For the game as a whole: shaders by
   how many renderers use them, with their passes (a lit pass is a draw call per renderer), post effects by script, and the
   QualitySettings levels. This tells you where to look before anything runs.
2. **A frame census on the handheld** (to do: built on `UA_GL_REC=census` and `UA_GL_TRACE_FILL`):
   each frame's draws grouped by what they are (shadow map, depth texture, opaque, outline, transparent,
   post, UI) with their GPU time and fill, so a 3D scene's cost splits into these rows. Unity's render
   target switches and the program's pass names (a ShadowCaster program, `_CameraDepthTexture`) tell the
   groups apart.
3. **Exact cuts** (picture and game play identical; each with a `verify` mode, as `UA_CULL_STEP1`):
   - a depth texture that nothing samples once the post effects that wanted it are gone;
   - shadow casters that are outside every cascade, and empty cascades;
   - redundant state and uniform uploads across a frame's many renderers (`UA_GL_STATE`, `UA_GL_MEMO`);
   - culling and skinning on the CPU (Unity's culling jobs, `UA_CULL_STEP1`; skinning in NEON);
   - the shaders' own costs on the Mali (`UA_GL_SPEC`'s twins: specialised for the uniforms a frame keeps).
4. **Reductions no one sees** (the Low picture; proven per option with `picdiff frames` over several
   scenes, the bar in quality.md): shadow map size and cascades, effects that cost a full-screen pass
   for a small change at 640x480 (AO, chromatic aberration, grain, motion blur), bloom's
   lowest-resolution chain steps, render size (`UA_RENDER_PIXELS`), the game's own lowest options.
   Never: LOD bias, shadow distance or far planes (they change what the player sees far away), anything
   a game's code reads back.
5. **Proof that the game play is the same**: the lockstep checker ([lockstep.md](lockstep.md)) for
   every CPU-side cut. A 3D game's risk is in what rendering feeds back into the game: animators set to cull
   (an off-screen animator that stops moving its bones changes root motion and physics), `OnBecameVisible`,
   renderer bounds read by the game. census3d lists the animators by culling mode for that reason.
   A cut that touches culling must leave those answers alone.
6. **A scene tour per game**: the same camera paths every run (a recorded input or save points), so the
   frame census, picdiff and fps numbers compare across builds.

## Bomb Rush Cyberfunk

census3d on the Steam build (2021.3.20f1, 2026-11-27; the handheld's numbers go here as they come):

- **Ten playable scene bundles** (prelude, hideout, square, tower, downhill, pyramid, mall, osaka, plus
  menus): 2900 - 13750 renderers each (with 1700 - 7000 more turned off), 1000 - 6900 LOD groups (one
  level each, mostly: LOD groups used for culling at a distance, not for simpler meshes), 1300 - 2800
  lightmapped, a third to a half static-batched, no occlusion culling data. Every scene has one
  mixed directional light (baked lightmaps, realtime shadows: soft in five scenes, hard in three); osaka
  and the hideout add 62 and 48 realtime point lights.
- **Nearly everything casts shadows**: 75 - 95 % of the renderers (osaka 27 %), and the environment
  shaders have no ShadowCaster pass of their own (their fallback's): the shadow map is a second draw of
  most of the scene.
- **The game's own shaders are one pass** ("Reptile/Ambient Environment", LightMode Always: no
  per-pixel lights, so the point lights cost the Standard materials only); characters are two
  ("Reptile/Ambient Character": Outline, then the body), and the GPU-instanced crowd shader three.
- **Characters and crowds**: 100 - 1750 skinned renderers per scene (pedestrians: 4200 Reptile.Pedestrian),
  1870 DynamicBone components (secondary motion on the CPU), 1100 animators always animated.
- **Post Processing Stack v2**: Color Grading, Depth of Field and Grain in the profiles, plus the game's
  own SunFlareGPU. Depth of field asks for the camera depth texture (another depth pass of the scene).
- **Cameras**: hundreds per scene (cutscenes' and the phone's; census3d reads the components, not whether their
  GameObjects are active), the gameplay ones with HDR on: half-float render targets, which on the Mali cost
  twice the bandwidth of 8-bit ones, before post processing turns them into the picture.
- **QualitySettings**: the game starts on Ultra (8x MSAA, four cascades of soft shadows, LOD bias 2);
  its options menu (VideoSettingsManager) sets the level and then shadow distance, resolution, quality,
  cascades, MSAA, anisotropic filtering and LOD bias by hand.
- **The game reads a quality setting back**: `StreetLifeCluster` halves the distance at which
  pedestrians and birds are put to sleep when `QualitySettings.lodBias < 2`. LOD bias stays 2 in the
  port's profile (the game's Low preset has 1): below 2 it changes the world simulation, not only the picture.

What this points to, to measure first on the G350: the shadow pass (most of the scene drawn twice),
draw calls (thousands of renderers, CPU-side culling of all of them on the A35s), skinning plus
DynamicBone, and the depth texture depth of field needs.
